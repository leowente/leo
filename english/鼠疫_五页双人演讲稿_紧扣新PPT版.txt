使用说明
两版任选一版，不要连续朗读。A负责第1—3页；B负责第4—5页。
A约224词；B约208词。按每分钟115—125词，加换页、指图及交接停顿，每人约2分钟，全程约4分钟。实际时间请以排练为准。
所有中文、方括号和页码均不朗读。将A和B换成自己的名字。
本版只围绕新PPT的画面、关键词和案例展开，不要求听众看过旧PPT。

[Speaker A · 第1页 · The plague · 约30秒]

Good morning, everyone. I'm A. Our topic today is plague. The mouse in this picture looks harmless. So why are we talking about a dangerous disease? Some wild animals and the fleas on them can carry the bacteria that cause plague. The picture introduces our question; it does not show an infected mouse. Let's learn what plague is and how it spreads.

[Speaker A · 第2页 · What is plague? How does it spread? · 约35秒]

On this slide, the key word is bacteria. Plague is caused by Yersinia pestis, not a virus. The three boxes show one common route. First, a flea feeds on an infected animal. Next, that flea carries the bacteria. Then it bites a person. There are two other routes: contact with infected animal tissues, and close contact with a person who has lung plague. That person can spread bacteria through cough droplets.

[Speaker A · 第3页 · Three main forms of plague · 约35秒，结束后交接]

This slide compares three forms. Bubonic plague mainly affects the lymph nodes, which become swollen and painful. Septicemic plague affects the blood and can cause shock or bleeding. Pneumonic plague affects the lungs and causes breathing problems. The blue message below is important: every form needs quick medical care. An untreated infection can spread to other parts of the body. People with plague may also develop a sudden fever, headache, and weakness. These signs alone do not prove someone has plague. My partner will now explain the history and the harm.

[Speaker B · 第4页 · Plague: then and now · 约50秒]

Thank you, A. I'm B. This slide takes us from the past to the present. The first example is the Black Death in the fourteenth century. It caused great loss of life in Europe. The second is northeast China in nineteen ten and nineteen eleven. About sixty thousand people died, and Doctor Wu Lien-teh helped lead the response. The third is Madagascar in twenty seventeen. By November twenty-second, over two thousand cases had been reported, including suspected cases. This recent outbreak reminds us that plague has not disappeared. It is still important to find cases and treat people early.

[Speaker B · 第5页 · Real outbreaks. Real harm. · 约55秒]

The picture shows an old town. It is an illustration, not a real photo of an outbreak. It helps us imagine how a serious disease can affect daily life. Beside the photo, we can see the main dangers. Plague can harm the lungs and blood, cause serious bleeding, and lead to death. It can also place a heavy burden on hospitals and families. However, the message at the bottom gives us hope: early antibiotics can save lives. We can reduce risk by avoiding flea bites and leaving sick or dead wild animals alone. After possible exposure, anyone who becomes ill should get medical help quickly. Thank you for listening.

排练提示
第1页：指向老鼠照片，不要把它说成感染鼠疫的老鼠。
第2页：按箭头指向动物、跳蚤和人。
第3页：疾病名称放慢读；结束后交给B。
第4页：数字用英文单词读法；“reported cases”包含疑似、可能和确诊病例。
第5页：指向历史场景插图，说明是示意图，然后讲危害和防治。
